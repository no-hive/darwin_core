LiquidDAO is a design for **organizations that can evolve**. It answers one question: how can we design modern DAOs so that people can leave them, together, without losing the recognition and voting power they have built?

The full design is in the [Liquid DAO whitepaper](https://no-hive.github.io/liquid_dao_v1) (MIT license, open to discussion and contribution). This page explains the concept in plain terms.

<Note>
Liquid DAO is a V1, a rough and evolving concept. A follow-up paper with clearer claims and a complete proposed code architecture is planned. Parts of this page will change with it.
</Note>

## The problem: leaving is costly

The more you contribute to an organization, the more costly leaving becomes. Money can move with you, but reputation and influence stay tied to the place you leave. That is where centralization takes hold.

The ability to separate is also a condition for evolution. Species, societies and organizations split to follow different paths, and those branches later meet and often reunite. DAOs without room to evolve risk becoming artifacts of their past.

Selling a token balance does not transfer your reputation, and buying a large balance does not make a newcomer a trusted voice.

## Voting Power and Score

In Liquid DAO, **Voting Power** is the broader ability to shape collective decisions: formal voting weight and the standing that makes others take your judgment seriously. Technically it is expressed as a **Score**: **Score = Voting Power**. In a one-token-one-vote DAO your token balance plays that role, here your Score does.

Every DAO decides how Scores are earned. Someone writes an application for the DAO, another provides liquidity, another does work that keeps the organization running. Each DAO defines how its Scores grow or decline: one may grant power quickly and reduce it after inactivity, another may require sustained contribution. The shared rules only define how organizations exist, relate and change, not how power must be earned.

## Recognition between DAOs

A DAO can use other DAOs, or other smart contracts, as **sources** of Voting Power. The receiving DAO chooses the source, the formula and the conditions for changing or removing the connection.

For example, a participant has a Score of 4 in a pool DAO. If a DEX DAO recognizes that Score at a weight of 0.5, it adds 2 to their DEX Score. If a network DAO then recognizes the DEX Score at a weight of 0.5, that adds 1 to their network Score. Voting Power of 4, 2 and 1 in three DAOs.

These connections form a directed graph. Connections can also run in both directions, which needs a rule that bounds the calculation: the paper considers a maximum recognition depth of three levels.

## Hierarchy, autonomy and diversity

An organization can belong to a larger one while keeping its own purpose, rules and participants. The larger whole governs shared undertakings, and its members stay autonomous in their own affairs. Authority stays distributed across levels. The resulting structure can include hierarchies, alliances and overlapping affiliations.

## Forking: the ability to leave

Being able to leave means being able to **branch from an existing DAO with the Voting Power its participants have already accumulated**.

A single fork command creates a mirror DAO from a governance snapshot, preserving Scores and the rules that produced them. The group creating the fork chooses the new branch's sources and formulas at that moment. No permission from the original DAO is needed.

An inherited minority can become a majority over time as participation under the new rules changes the distribution of Scores. The inherited balance gives continuity without permanently fixing who holds power.

## In Darwin Core Protocol

In Darwin Core Protocol, the groups that form around findings are the DAOs. LiquidDAO lets them recognize one another, so that people who contribute in one group can carry that standing into others, and groups can split without starting from nothing. Groups are meant to be able to merge as well as split.

The voting system of the prototype is built on the basis of Agora and LiquidDAO. See [Project State](/project-state) for what exists today and the [Voting stack](/technical/voting-stack) for implementation.
